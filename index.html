📚 Frequently Asked Questions

Citizen Rights, Grievance Rules & Legal Procedures

Answers to key questions regarding Gram Panchayat authority, time-bound legal resolution under UP Janhit Guarantee Act, and RTI applications.

How to lodge an official complaint against a Gram Pradhan or Panchayat Secretary in Uttar Pradesh? ▾

Under the UP Panchayati Raj Act, 1947, the supervisory administrative officer for a Gram Panchayat is the Block Development Officer (BDO) and the District Panchayat Raj Officer (DPRO). You can draft a formal petition addressed to the BDO with auto-CC to the District Magistrate (DM) and Chief Development Officer (CDO), record a physical receipt diary number, and follow up via official CUG phone.

What is the statutory resolution timeframe for civic grievances under the UP Janhit Guarantee Act, 2011? ▾

Under the Uttar Pradesh Janhit Guarantee Act, 2011, basic public services (drinking water handpump repair, drainage clearing, brick road paving) carry a statutory timeframe, typically between 7 to 30 days. If an officer fails to act, citizens can escalate to the First Appellate Authority (SDM / CDO) and present the matter on Tehsil Samadhan Diwas.

When and how should an RTI Form-A application be filed for civic works in UP? ▾

RTI (Right to Information) is not a grievance redressal mechanism, but a transparency tool. If your grievance is unresolved after 30 days, file RTI Form-A seeking certified copies of the Measurement Book (MB), technical sanction estimates, tender sanctions, and Action Taken Reports (ATR) to establish evidence of administrative inaction or financial irregularities.

How does multi-department Auto-CC to DM, CDO, and SDM ensure action? ▾

Marking copies (CC) to supervisory authorities creates an official administrative trail. Under UP administrative protocols, field-level officers (BDOs/EOs) must report compliance to the District Magistrate and CDO during weekly review meetings, preventing field petitions from being silently ignored.
